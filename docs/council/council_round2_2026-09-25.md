## Phase-2 results skeptic (results-skeptic)

I found a material baseline weakness: **a simple, model-free pause-filling rule recovers the entire headline gain.** Filling gaps of at most five seconds between zero-shot segments assigned to the same speaker gives **27.56% DER**, versus the registered fine-tuned **28.69%**. This is a **post hoc outer-fold diagnostic, not a replacement verdict**. The registered 42.75 → 28.69 comparison remains reproducible; its interpretation as a substantial advantage over a sufficiently calibrated zero-shot system is much weaker.

The strongest counterexample uses only predicted speaker identities and segment times. I swept gap limits of 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2, 3, 5 and 10 seconds, pooling **only each fold’s eight inner recordings** for selection. Filling same-speaker gaps up to five seconds won in all five pools. Inner zero-shot DER changed, in F1–F5 order, from **43.12/42.19/40.59/44.49/43.42** to **29.63/26.75/24.05/29.49/28.00**. The registered fine-tuned inner scores were **28.28/25.84/23.86/28.34/26.66**. Thus a decoder change alone recovers 13.49–16.54 points on inner recordings. Even a two-second limit produces inner DER of 26.23–31.66%. I verified that all 40 corresponding zero-shot inner RTTMs are byte-identical to the frozen zero-shot predictions used in these calculations. [Inner gap results](/tmp/bnd_phase2_challenge/gap_inner.json), [registered selections](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/bengali-diarization-nemotron/runs/cv/selection.json).

**Outer diagnostic only:** pyannote independently confirmed the five-second zero-shot result as **27.5582 [MISS 6.9091 / FA 6.8503 / CONF 13.7988]**. It improves all ten recordings over the original zero-shot output. Fine-tuned minus this alternative baseline is **+1.13 pp**, with the same recording-bootstrap procedure giving **95% CI [−1.90, +4.47]**, five wins and five losses. Applying the identical pause-filling rule to fine-tuned output gives **28.50%**; choosing its gap limit independently on its inner predictions gives **28.57%**. The symmetric strict comparison becomes **27.62% zero-shot versus 27.53% fine-tuned**, delta **−0.09 pp [−0.96, +1.01]**. These intervals do not establish equivalence or zero-shot superiority, but they remove evidence of a large residual fine-tuning advantage against this control. The smoothed selected zero-shot also scores **25.71% at collar 0.25 and 23.91% at collar 0.5**, so this counterexample is not confined to collar zero. [Validated outer diagnostics](/tmp/bnd_phase2_challenge/gap_outer_validation.json), [collar diagnostics](/tmp/bnd_phase2_challenge/gap_outer_collars.json).

The activity-grid floor is also a real weakness, though a smaller one in the isolated experiment. The 0.40 reconstruction exactly reproduced the existing RTTMs. A sweep down through the smallest positive float16 value, including zero, selected thresholds **0.0001/0.0005/0.0005/0.001/0.0005** on the five inner pools, yielding **39.01/38.03/35.97/41.18/39.52% DER**: improvements of **3.31–4.61 pp**. Threshold 0.10 alone recovers **1.36–1.65 pp**. These are **activity-only sensitivities**, not a complete lower-threshold embedding-and-clustering search: threshold changes also alter the embedding crops under [infer.py:208](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/bengali-diarization-nemotron/bnd/infer.py:208). Consequently, this quantifies recoverable baseline weakness without identifying the best fully retuned zero-shot system. [Threshold calculations and methods](/tmp/bnd_phase2_challenge/challenge_summary.json).

## Phase-3 linking design critic (linking-design-critic)

I would amend phase 3 before running it. The search targets the right subsystem, but its emphasis on small-cluster absorption and more permissive merging misses most of the error duration. A modest improvement is plausible; the inner evidence does not support expecting this grid to recover a large fraction of the 11.34-point oracle-linking gap. The main problems are substantial fragments composed of strong tracks, incorrect mergers of different speakers, and constraints that sometimes prevent correct reunions.

I made no filesystem changes and used no GPU. All new measurements below use phase-2 **inner predictions only**, following each fold’s selected recipe and cell in [selection.json](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/bengali-diarization-nemotron/runs/cv/selection.json:18). I checked that each prediction’s fit excluded both the relevant outer fold and the recording being diagnosed. This gives 40 recording–fit observations, representing ten recordings four times—not 40 independent recordings. I did not score any phase-3 candidate. For error attribution, I reconstructed the existing exclusive output and computed Hungarian-mapped confusion on frames with exactly one reference speaker. These are diagnostic confusion seconds, not replacements for registered overlap-inclusive DER.

**The weak-track hypothesis is largely wrong.** Across those observations, 520 of 12,415 tracks were weak, but they contributed only 131 seconds of output and 101 of 13,243 diagnostic confusion seconds: **0.76% of confusion**. Tracks with at least four seconds of usable embedding audio contributed **93.3% of confusion**. This holds within F1’s eight inner recordings too: weak tracks contributed only 27.49 of 3,116.80 confusion seconds. Improving weak attachment may clean up speaker counts, but cannot directly explain a substantial DER gain.

**Small clusters dominate the count of spurious labels, not their error duration.** Defining a spurious-label candidate as an output cluster unmatched by the optimal one-to-one reference mapping, their counts by owned-core duration were: below 1 second, 173; 1–5 seconds, 250; 5–15 seconds, 118; 15–60 seconds, 59; above 60 seconds, 17. Thus 88% were below 15 seconds, but the 76 larger clusters contained **74% of unmatched-cluster confusion**. Across *all* output clusters, those below 15 seconds contained only **12.4% of confusion**; those above 60 seconds contained **68.5%**. This is not a strict upper bound on absorption’s benefit, because merges can change the global mapping, but it shows where its direct intervention is concentrated. There is also real minority-speaker risk: **61 of 147 reference speakers in F1’s inner set have less than 15 seconds of reference speech**. Duration alone cannot distinguish erroneous fragments from genuine short appearances.

Two concrete examples show why the distinction matters. In F1-inner `train_006`, internal cluster 10 has **203.43 seconds of output from 21 strong tracks**, including 181.73 seconds of reference speaker `3`; it is nevertheless unmatched and contributes 184.72 confusion seconds. Neither absorption setting touches it. Conversely, in F1-inner `train_011`, internal cluster 13 contains **1,006.06 seconds from 85 strong tracks**, including 650.03 seconds of speaker `11` and 246.85 seconds of speaker `18`. This needs separation or reassignment, not another merger. These calculations use the existing assignments in the [train_006 link report](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/bengali-diarization-nemotron/runs/cv/hyp/cv_inner_x15_lr5e-5_raw/0.40_0.55/link_report.json:708) and [train_011 link report](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/bengali-diarization-nemotron/runs/cv/hyp/cv_inner_x12_lr5e-5_raw/0.40_0.55/link_report.json:964).

An algebraic decomposition reinforces this mixed diagnosis. Allowing each individual track its best reference identity leaves 2,723 diagnostic confusion seconds attributable to local-track impurity. Replacing individual tracks with current clusters adds **4,493 seconds of within-cluster mixing**; imposing the final one-to-one speaker mapping adds another **6,027 seconds of fragmentation/mapping loss**. Thus approximately **43% of the linking-attributable loss is mixing and 57% fragmentation** under this diagnostic. Heavy over-counting does not imply that uniformly stronger merging is the solution.

**WeSpeaker does separate speakers in this audio.** I labelled embeddings from their actual crop intervals, retaining strong tracks whose crops had at least 80% reference coverage and 90% reference-speaker purity. This retained 8,582 tracks. For cross-window pairs at least 300 seconds apart, same-speaker cosine distance had median **0.214**, versus **0.835** for different speakers; pairwise discrimination AUC was **0.945**. Restricting both tracks to at least four usable seconds increased AUC to **0.964**. Among eligible tracks with a same-speaker example at least 300 seconds away, nearest-neighbour identity accuracy was **93.8%**. These are descriptive, reference-filtered diagnostics; they favour clean tracks and frequent speakers. Nevertheless, they contradict wholesale embedding failure on Bengali drama.

The difficulty is heterogeneous within-speaker variation. Within F1’s inner set, distant same-speaker distances had median/90th percentile **0.552/0.803 on train_006**, **0.493/0.824 on train_002**, but **0.164/0.370 on train_011**. Across all distant pairs, moving the distance threshold from 0.55 to 0.75 increases same-speaker acceptance from 81.3% to 94.2%, while increasing different-speaker acceptance from **4.8% to 30.4%**. These pairwise rates are not AHC merge rates, but explain the tradeoff. The existing inner DER tables already confirm it: raising θ_clu from 0.55 to 0.65 at the selected θ_act worsens DER by **0.31–2.08 points in F1/F3/F4/F5**; F2 already selected 0.65. Interpolation at 0.60 is reasonable. Broadly encouraging mergers is not.

The hard same-window constraint is another material limitation. Among clean embedding-labelled tracks, I found **355 same-reference-speaker pairs assigned different channels within one window**, including **261 pairs with less than 0.1 seconds of overlapping track activity**. These are repeated-fit observations, not unique events. The [current constraint](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/bengali-diarization-nemotron/bnd/link.py:15) forbids their reunion and propagates that prohibition through subsequent merges. Among small clusters with a larger cluster dominated by the same reference speaker, roughly one-third were blocked from joining that larger cluster by shared window membership. Raising θ_clu, absorption, and overlap hints all preserve this obstruction.

Overlap correspondence remains worth reporting, but looks mostly redundant on clean tracks. The registered phase-3 hint rule generated 7,781 hints on the existing inner caches. Of 5,170 whose endpoints passed the crop-quality filter, **5,161 agreed with the reference identity**, but only **124 correctly connected tracks currently assigned to different clusters**. That is promising precision with limited demonstrated incremental coverage. Impure endpoints remain uncertain, and transitive effects could be larger; these counts alone do not establish a DER benefit.

I propose **three amendments**, replacing search dimensions rather than adding another large Cartesian product.

**Amendment 1: replace the absorption sweep with one fixed cluster-then-reassign alternative that can revise strong tracks and large clusters.** Use tracks with at least four usable seconds to form conservative seed clusters; merge compatible normalized centroids without a cluster-duration cutoff, then run two deterministic track-reassignment passes with a fixed nearest-versus-runner-up margin and abstention. Short tracks should attach when supported, without automatically deleting short-speaker clusters. In this alternative, make cannot-link depend on actual confident coactivity rather than merely sharing a window; retain the original constrained AHC as a control. Freeze these details before scoring. This is CPU-only with existing embeddings. Its expected advantage is access to the substantial strong-track fragmentation and mixing identified above. Its risk is merging sequential speakers or reinforcing a bad seed; the good distant-neighbour discrimination supports testing it, not assuming it will work. The existing `θ_clu + 0.15` absorption rule also compares normalized-centroid distance with an average-linkage threshold, which are different distance distributions; that offset has no demonstrated calibration.

**Amendment 2: test one longer native session length—80-second owned cores with five-second context.** The current implementation calls `model.forward` independently for every window in [infer.py](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/bengali-diarization-nemotron/bnd/infer.py:413), and native streaming initializes a fresh state on every call in [Sortformer](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/bengali-diarization-nemotron/third_party/nemo-speech/nemo/collections/asr/models/sortformer_diar_models.py:1106). Consequently, ≤30-second inputs give the speaker cache little opportunity to preserve identity across successive native chunks. The model’s documented purpose for AOSC is precisely to retain earlier speaker information. [NVIDIA model card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/Nemotron-3-Diarization). Fixed 80-second cores reduce external reset boundaries about fourfold. As a capacity check—not a deployment rule—none of the 438 resulting sessions across recordings used as inner data contained more than eight reference speakers; `train_006` reached eight. This requires inference and embeddings, but no training. Risks are increased within-track impurity and speaker-capacity saturation, so compare local-track oracle confusion as well as linked DER.

The longer-session experiment appears affordable if it replaces most of the threshold expansion. Apportioning existing inner inference timings by audio duration gives roughly **656 seconds of Nemotron work plus 310 seconds for one embedding threshold** for the selected inner predictions. For example, [one four-recording inference report](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/bengali-diarization-nemotron/runs/cv/cache/cv_inner_x12_lr5e-5_raw/infer_report.json:207) records 62.9 seconds of Nemotron and 92.9 seconds of embedding work for three thresholds. Longer-session throughput is unmeasured; budget it with a fixed ceiling, for example 0.75 GPU-hours including the zero-shot comparison and necessary outer inference, preserving the remaining allocation for final training and contingencies. I would not introduce full VBx within this budget: the official implementation requires PLDA and embedding transforms, whose compatibility with these cached WeSpeaker vectors has not been established. [VBx implementation](https://github.com/BUTSpeechFIT/VBx/blob/master/VBx/vbhmm.py).

**Amendment 3: reduce and regularize selection, and make the final-default rule explicit.** One concrete replacement is to retain each fold’s phase-2 θ_act and compare θ_clu `{0.55, 0.60, 0.65}`, three linker alternatives `{avg, avg+ov, refinement}`, and two core lengths `{20, 80}`: **18 candidates**, including every phase-2 baseline. Prefer the baseline or simpler candidate when its paired inner loss is within one recording-level standard error of the minimum; preregister the exact calculation and tie order. Report leave-one-recording-out selection stability. For the final configuration, a preregistered joint mode of the five inner selections, with deterministic conservative ties, would avoid another exhaustive outer-label minimization. This amendment costs essentially no GPU time.

The overfitting concern is measurable already, before 150-way selection. Resampling the eight recordings 10,000 times using only the existing **15-cell** inner tables, with recipe held fixed, recovered the original winning cell only **50%, 48%, 73%, 37%, and 50%** of the time for F1–F5. Removing one recording changed the winner in four of five folds. Runner-up margins were only **0.31–0.49 points in four folds**. These are stability diagnostics, not additional validation estimates. Exact tie-breaks do little to control this instability; including the baseline guarantees non-worsening selected *inner* loss by construction.

There are three further interpretation problems in the present rules. First, “linker only” is inaccurate: [θ_act changes activity and embedding selection together](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/bengali-diarization-nemotron/bnd/infer.py:208). A gain at 0.25 could chiefly reduce misses, increase false alarms, or change crop purity. The 17.35% oracle result is conditional on phase-2 tracks and speech decisions; it is not a fixed endpoint for all new activity thresholds. Component reporting and an oracle computed from each selected system’s own tracks are essential.

Second, the [gate](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/bengali-diarization-nemotron/docs/PREREG_phase3.md:45) is a reasonable operational safeguard, but its bootstrap interval is conditional on these reused recordings and fitted systems. It does not restore independence after outer-informed search design, nor include model-selection/training uncertainty. The preregistration’s “second-look” disclosure is appropriate and must accompany any success claim. The gate also lacks phase 2’s macro-mean condition; it can pass while average per-recording DER worsens, despite six recordings improving.

Third, [the final-model rule](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/bengali-diarization-nemotron/docs/PREREG_phase3.md:50) selects a fresh configuration on all ten outer labels after the gate. Calling that a development choice is correct, but the gate does **not** validate that particular configuration or the final all-ten model. Its tuning predictions also mix F1’s raw recipe with four shifted recipes, whereas the final model is shifted throughout and receives fewer effective passes. “Otherwise P2’s linker stays” is ambiguous when failure still triggers fresh selection across the D1 grid: retaining the algorithm family and retaining the evaluated configuration are different commitments.

The main dependencies are implementation of the fixed refinement, session-length support with distinct cache identities, and a preregistration amendment before candidate scores exist. Reference-label quality and the clean-crop filtering limit the acoustic conclusions; I did not listen to adjudicate whether particularly similar reference speakers are annotation aliases. Open owner decisions are whether to relax the fixed-window contract and whether “default” means the evaluated selection procedure or a subsequently retuned final artifact. Neither requires additional training to resolve the proposed linking experiments.
