## More-than-8-speaker long-form linking design (long-form-linking)

There are material issues in this inference path. My primary recommendation is **short, overlapping inference windows with constrained recording-level clustering**, using overlap correspondence to strengthen linking. My fallback is **bounded AOSC streaming sessions, reset on a fixed schedule, followed by the same global linker**. Do not run one uninterrupted eight-channel session over a recording containing 10–27 speakers and interpret its channels as recording-global identities. Unlimited streaming duration does not remove the speaker-capacity limit. These recommendations assume offline processing and permission to finalize identities after reading the whole recording; they are design choices awaiting recording-level validation, not measured winners.

I inspected the supplied files and verified that their workspace copies have identical hashes. I ran targeted CPU reproductions against the actual postprocessing functions; [the output is saved here](/tmp/nemotron_inference_audit/check_postprocess.out). I did not run Nemotron or an embedding model on a GPU, and I did not access evaluation recordings.

The API concern needs correction. Calling `model.forward(audio_signal=..., audio_signal_length=...)` is not inherently wrong: the inspected NeMo implementation routes it through `forward_streaming` when streaming mode is enabled, and `diarize()` ultimately calls that same forward method. However, `forward_streaming` initializes a new state on every call. Consequently, the sample’s repeated window calls discard AOSC history between windows. Merely replacing `forward()` with separate `diarize()` calls would preserve that reset behavior. This is verified against NeMo commit `cf724ac337d1ebc7d0dda1e23fb80916f52927a5`; the eventual environment must pin and test its actual source revision. See the [upstream implementation](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NVIDIA-NeMo/Speech/cf724ac337d1ebc7d0dda1e23fb80916f52927a5/nemo/collections/asr/models/sortformer_diar_models.py).

There is an additional consequence of the sample’s configuration: a native chunk spans 340 × 80 ms = 27.2 seconds, while an interior window’s emitted core occupies window-relative seconds 5–25. Thus its emitted core lies inside the first native chunk, which starts with an empty cache. The nominal streaming configuration gives those core predictions effectively no preceding-chunk AOSC history. This follows from the inspected chunk loader and window geometry; it is not a measured accuracy finding.

The window/core arithmetic itself is sound. [postprocess.py:9](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/bengali-diarization-nemotron/reference/sample_l4/src/postprocess.py:9) partitions samples exactly once. [infer.py:80](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/bengali-diarization-nemotron/reference/sample_l4/src/infer.py:80) correctly clips local turns to `(core_start − window_start, core_end − window_start)` without subtracting that offset, then restores `window_start` when emitting absolute timestamps. My checks covered six recording durations, including 90 minutes plus a partial tail, and verified an interior-window example. I found no double-offset or duplicated-core defect.

What is missing from that bookkeeping is provenance. The cache retains only embeddings and `(track_index, start, end)` rows. It loses window IDs, local channel IDs, context activity, posterior confidence and embedding quality. This prevents enforcing window constraints later, prevents overlap-based matching, and makes many decoder experiments require another GPU pass. Preserve a window table and track table, plus raw posteriors, their exact frame spacing, valid lengths and absolute origins. Context-only tracks can provide linking evidence even when they emit no core speech; currently they are discarded.

Frame timing is an integration risk rather than a demonstrated eightfold bug. [infer.py:27](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/bengali-diarization-nemotron/reference/sample_l4/src/infer.py:27) uses the model’s **output** subsampling factor, which is the appropriate distinction. Do not substitute the encoder’s factor of eight: NVIDIA documents an 80 ms encoder rate with configurable returned predictions, including 10 ms. The current guard checks only substantially short outputs; an incorrect, overly large `dt` could pass and silently misplace speech. Test actual returned lengths and timestamp alignment on short, exact-chunk, partial-chunk and padded inputs, and compare the wrapper against native inference using identical settings. [NVIDIA model card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/Nemotron-3-Diarization)

The earliest-onset decoder has reproducible failure modes. In [postprocess.py:19](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/bengali-diarization-nemotron/reference/sample_l4/src/postprocess.py:19), a channel at probability 0.51 throughout ten seconds defeats another channel at 0.99 from seconds two through eight: the output is entirely the earlier channel. This is the algorithm’s intended rule, but a serious robustness problem when a false positive remains above threshold. Simultaneous predicted onsets are more concretely problematic: their tie is reconsidered from instantaneous probability every frame. Alternating confidence at 10 ms produced alternating labels, then **no output speech** after the 100 ms minimum-turn filter.

Earliest predicted onset is also left-censored at every window boundary. Two speakers already active when a window starts receive identical onset times regardless of who actually began first. Threshold dips reset an onset, and short-turn filtering deletes speech without reassignment. These facts mean the decoder is only a heuristic approximation to the annotation policy. Sortformer’s channel ordering by a speaker’s first arrival does not establish the onset order of every subsequent overlapping utterance.

Keep multi-channel activity until after identity linking, and derive the required single-label output separately. My initial decoder would use confidence-based winner selection with hysteresis and conservative temporal smoothing, with the onset-aware alternative included in training-fold comparisons. If earliest-onset decoding survives that comparison, maintain onset state across linked boundaries and resolve ties consistently over an activity episode. Do not use the single-label projection to decide which audio is acoustically uncontaminated for embeddings: an interval assigned to one speaker can still contain another speaker’s voice.

Embedding extraction needs substantial revision. [infer.py:35](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/bengali-diarization-nemotron/reference/sample_l4/src/infer.py:35) chooses only the three longest turns, crops their centers to at most three seconds, and averages normalized embeddings by duration. It does not exclude predicted overlap, inspect speaker confidence, handle inconsistent crops, or abstain from weak evidence. A mislabeled long turn can dominate its track representation; background music or a second voice remains in the waveform despite exclusive labels.

For subsecond turns, the code pads to one second and passes that padded length as `input_signal_length`. Thus the embedder receives the padding as valid-duration input. Duration weighting afterwards cannot undo its effect on feature normalization or pooling. Padding may be necessary for an implementation’s minimum input size, but it does not create identity evidence. Use valid lengths or masks where supported; otherwise treat the short-input behavior explicitly as something to validate.

Prefer several clean, temporally separated crops with roughly 1.5–4 seconds of actual speech, when available, and retain their individual embeddings and disagreement statistics. Select from confident, non-overlapping activity, with modest boundary exclusion calibrated on training data. Tracks lacking enough usable speech should remain provisional and obtain identity from a reliable overlapping-window correspondence or later evidence. They should not seed unrestricted cluster merges. Preserve unmatched short turns as separate provisional identities instead of forcing a nearest-speaker assignment or deleting them.

The missing cannot-link constraints are confirmed. [postprocess.py:64](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/bengali-diarization-nemotron/reference/sample_l4/src/postprocess.py:64) performs ordinary average-linkage clustering and has no window information. My reproduction assigned two similar vectors, designated as different tracks of the same window, to the same cluster. Distinct local channels need an explicit cannot-link relation if the linker is to preserve the diarizer’s local distinctions. Enforce it at **every cluster merge**: two clusters may merge only if their union contains no forbidden pair. Merely inflating the original pairwise distance or removing a connectivity edge is insufficient protection against indirect merges.

This constraint is a modeling assumption, not guaranteed truth. A diarizer can split one real speaker across two local channels; hard cannot-link then preserves that mistake. Measure this failure mode explicitly. A constrained baseline is still preferable to silently allowing the linker to erase every local distinction, but compare hard constraints against a controlled relaxation only on training folds.

The fixed 0.35 threshold is unsupported calibration, not a mathematical error. It is a **cosine-distance** threshold; the corresponding pairwise cosine similarity is 0.65. Average linkage thresholds cluster-average distances, so this does not imply every merged pair has similarity above 0.65. Its behavior changes with embedding model, crop duration, segmentation quality, pooling, constraints and linkage method. No value from another model card or paper should be copied into this pipeline as an operating threshold.

There are smaller reproducibility defects too. [infer.py:147](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/bengali-diarization-nemotron/reference/sample_l4/src/infer.py:147) fingerprints the embedder using the stored lock checksum rather than rehashing the file loaded for this run. It does not explicitly include the active NeMo revision, effective streaming settings or output resolution. The summary stores one mutable identity object alongside multiple recording results, leaving only the latest recording’s audio hash in that object. Store immutable per-recording provenance and effective runtime settings.

For the primary implementation, retain 20-second cores with five seconds of context on either side as the **initial candidate**, not as a demonstrated optimum. These yield at most 30-second inputs and ten seconds of shared audio between adjacent interior windows. Run native inference independently per window, assign every channel a `(recording, window, channel)` identity, and retain both core and context activity. Keep exact sample-based ownership of output cores. Short windows limit how much speaker diversity each eight-channel inference must represent while retaining a straightforward, reference-free schedule.

Thirty seconds does not guarantee eight or fewer distinct speakers. Count distinct reference speakers in the **entire input window, including context**, on training recordings only; the relevant capacity is not merely simultaneous speech. Compare a small preregistered set of shorter windows if capacity overflow is material. An inference-time “all eight channels used” signal can flag suspicious regions, but it cannot reliably detect an unseen ninth speaker that was already missed or merged. No schedule should claim a formal capacity guarantee.

Use adjacent-window overlap to propose correspondences by aligning activity in absolute time and solving a partial one-to-one assignment with unmatched options. Require meaningful shared speech and an adequate score margin; silence agreement should contribute no identity evidence. Combine temporal agreement with embeddings where available. Permit abstention on ambiguous matches, and reject any correspondence that creates a cannot-link conflict. Avoid propagating one uncertain match through a long chain as an unquestioned identity.

Then run constrained global clustering over the recording’s reliable track representations, using conservative overlap correspondences as additional evidence. Global embedding links remain necessary when a character disappears and returns much later. Assign weak tracks afterwards using constrained matching with a new-identity option. Remap local activity to global identities, reconstruct each owned core once, apply the selected exclusive decoder, and coalesce touching same-speaker turns. Do not impose the recording’s true speaker count or a target of 10–27 clusters.

This combines the strengths of fixed short windows and overlapping-window stitching. **Pure overlap stitching is insufficient**: a returning speaker absent from all intervening overlaps cannot be identified by overlap continuity alone. Its principal advantage is matching the same acoustic event twice, which can rescue brief turns that do not support useful standalone embeddings. Its main danger is erroneous transitive matching. The global linker must therefore remain able to abstain and enforce conflicts.

The fallback is native AOSC inference over **60-second cores with five-second boundary context**, resetting the complete cache/FIFO state for each session and linking session-local tracks afterwards. Compare this initial candidate with 120-second cores only if training-recording speaker occupancy permits. Multiple native chunks then share state, giving recurring speakers more accumulated evidence and reducing the number of track fragments. All channel namespaces restart at a session reset; channel zero in successive sessions has no automatic identity relationship.

The fallback’s central risk is accumulated speaker diversity: nine speakers appearing sequentially within a session can exceed capacity even if only two speak at once. It can therefore improve short-turn continuity while worsening irreversible local speaker mergers. Prefer it only if training-fold ablations show lower local error and an acceptable overflow rate. A longer uninterrupted cache is not automatically better for drama with scene changes.

Using Nemotron’s own cache representations should remain an experimental third path. The inspected cache stores selected **pre-encoder frame representations**, associated activity predictions and silence-related entries; it is not an exposed bank of independently trained speaker-verification vectors. See [cache construction](/tmp/nemotron_inference_audit/sortformer_modules.py:1288) and [streaming update](/tmp/nemotron_inference_audit/sortformer_diar_models.py:1505). Pooling appropriately masked representations by local speaker is a reasonable experiment and may avoid a second network, but cross-session cosine comparability is unproven. Evaluate which layer to pool, remove padding/silence, and recalibrate separately for zero-shot and fine-tuned checkpoints. These representations neither increase the eight-channel capacity nor repair a channel already containing multiple speakers.

For the initial embedder, I recommend **`pyannote/wespeaker-voxceleb-resnet34-LM`**, with a pinned revision and the documented frontend. It has accessible weights under CC-BY-4.0 and an established embedding interface. More specifically, a published Bengali-Loop competition system used this exact embedding family with adapted segmentation, giving it relevant Bangla-domain precedent. That is evidence for testing it, not an isolated demonstration that it beats TitaNet in this system. Do not import that paper’s clustering threshold or reported DER as an expected result. [Model card](https://huggingface.co/pyannote/wespeaker-voxceleb-resnet34-LM), [Bangla system paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.21741v1)

Its selection in [models.py:14](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/bengali-diarization-nemotron/reference/sample_l4/src/models.py:14) simply has no in-domain justification yet. The most useful falsification measurement is **oracle linking with predicted local activity held fixed**. Replace only track-to-global assignments using reference identities; do not repair boundaries, suppress false alarms or split mixed-speaker tracks. High oracle-linked DER exposes local segmentation, decoding, capacity collisions or impure tracks that global clustering cannot repair. Do not create a cannot-link between every individual reference turn: repeated turns by the same local speaker must form one local track. ## Validation and DER evaluation protocol (eval-protocol)

Use **nested five-fold recording-level validation, followed by a separate all-10 fit**. A single 8/2 split is suitable for engineering checks but too fragile for the principal comparison. Leave-one-recording-out would train on nine recordings, but doubles the outer fits without increasing the ten independent evaluation units. Five disjoint two-recording folds provide a reasonable compromise. Material scoring and decoder issues exist; the current implementation should not yet support an improvement claim.

I saved a [proposed preregistration](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/bengali-diarization-nemotron/audits/validation-2026-09-25/proposed-registration.json), [synthetic audit results](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/bengali-diarization-nemotron/audits/validation-2026-09-25/synthetic-audit.json), and [submission-format evidence](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/bengali-diarization-nemotron/audits/validation-2026-09-25/submission-audit.json). These are prospective decisions, not evidence of model performance. I ran no GPU training and computed no empirical Bengali-Loop DER.

Assuming the ten recordings are independent source items, freeze these outer folds: F1 = `001,008`; F2 = `011,009`; F3 = `013,005`; F4 = `004,002`; F5 = `003,006`, all with the `train_` prefix. These come from a fixed shuffle with seed `20260925`. Check source provenance and duplicate excerpts first. Excerpts from the same original recording must share a fold, even if that changes fold sizes. Recording-held-out evaluation does not establish speaker-disjoint evaluation: recurring actors may appear across recordings.

For outer fold Fi, its two recordings are completely excluded from fitting and calibration. Within the remaining eight, rotate each other two-recording fold Fj as inner validation, training the fine-tuned model on the remaining six. Generate full-recording predictions for Fj, select thresholds using the eight pooled inner out-of-fold recordings, then refit from the original pretrained checkpoint on all eight outer-training recordings. Apply the selected thresholds to Fi. Complete and freeze all five outer prediction sets before inspecting their comparative scores. Training on eight and tuning on the same outer two would not be nested validation.

Allow exactly **nine postprocessing candidates per system**: activity threshold `{0.40, 0.50, 0.60}` crossed with cosine-distance clustering threshold `{0.25, 0.35, 0.45}`. Select separately for each system by pooled inner DER at the primary collar, summing error seconds and reference-speech seconds before dividing. Each system receives the same candidates, recordings, metric, and selection opportunities: 36 candidate/fold evaluations per outer fold. Resolve numerical ties within `1e-8` DER percentage points by proximity to `(0.50,0.35)`, then ascending activity threshold and clustering threshold. No grid expansion after seeing results.

Freeze every other choice before scoring: embedding checkpoint, linking algorithm and constraints, window/session geometry, onset tie policy, minimum turn duration, gap filling, timestamp offsets or rounding, preprocessing, augmentation, and model configuration. Keep minimum turn duration at `0.10 s` for this proposed experiment and disable additional gap filling. Collar, UEM, overlap exclusion, and oracle speaker counts are evaluation definitions, never tunable hyperparameters. Changing the activity threshold changes the predicted embedding crops in this implementation, so recompute the corresponding embeddings; do not reuse crops generated at another threshold.

To make equal tuning budgets meaningful, use **one fixed fine-tuning recipe**, with no learning-rate, checkpoint, epoch, or seed search. My proposed starting recipe is `top8`, AdamW, learning rate `1e-5`, betas `(0.9,0.98)`, weight decay `0.001`, inverse-square-root scheduling with 25 warmup steps and minimum learning rate `1e-6`, batch size 1, accumulation 8, gradient clipping 1.0, bf16, seed 42, and exactly three epochs. Export the final checkpoint; remove validation-loss early stopping and the 1,000-step cap. Freeze the remaining resolved model settings and augmentation behavior. This is an experimental prescription, not a claim that these settings are optimal; the training/API council work must verify feasibility before registration is finalized.

There are 20 logical six-recording inner fits, five eight-recording outer fits, and one all-10 final fit. With a fixed recipe and seed, inner fits can share checkpoints when their six-recording training sets are identical: only ten distinct six-recording subsets occur. That reduces this to 16 physical training runs without changing the protocol. Every fit must start from the pinned pretrained model, not another fold’s adapted checkpoint.

Register two distinct comparisons. The primary operational comparison is **frozen weights with train-fold-calibrated postprocessing versus fine-tuned weights with equally calibrated postprocessing**. Calling the first system strictly zero-shot would be misleading because its thresholds used Bengali labels. Separately report strict zero-shot versus fine-tuned inference with identical fixed thresholds `(0.50,0.35)` and otherwise identical processing. That comparison has zero threshold-selection trials on either side and better isolates the effect of weight adaptation. Do not compare a nine-candidate fine-tuned winner only against an untuned baseline and describe the budgets as equal.

After locking the outer evaluation report, choose each system’s final thresholds using the same nine candidates on full ten-recording out-of-fold predictions from the five eight-recording models. Then train one fresh fine-tuned model on all ten recordings for the fixed three epochs. These final threshold-selection scores are development scores. The nested outer result estimates the registered procedure trained on eight recordings; it is not an independent score for the final all-10 checkpoint. Do not choose the best-performing fold checkpoint or introduce an ensemble after inspecting results.

For local scoring, register primary `DiarizationErrorRate(collar=0.25, skip_overlap=False)`, with collar explicitly described as **total excluded width**. Report `collar=0`, `0.5`, and `1.0` as prespecified sensitivities, using unchanged hypotheses and thresholds. They correspond to no collar, ±0.25 seconds, and ±0.5 seconds respectively. Never select whichever collar produces the strongest improvement. Use one Hungarian mapping for each complete recording and each scoring configuration, with UEM `[0, audio_samples/sample_rate)`, including silence. This assumes the references cover the whole file; an official restricted UEM, if discovered before registration, would supersede that assumption.

For recording i, retain scored reference-speech duration \(R_i\), MISS, FA, CONF, both DERs, and the paired difference \(d_i=DER_{FT,i}-DER_{base,i}\), in percentage points. Negative means improvement. Report corpus DER as `100 × sum(MISS+FA+CONF)/sum(R)`, its paired difference, macro mean DER and mean paired difference, median difference, wins/ties/losses, and the largest regression. Include recording duration, reference and predicted speaker counts, and selected fold thresholds. The corpus result is weighted by scored reference speech, not total audio duration. Reporting only an average of five fold DERs would weight recordings incorrectly.

Use a **paired recording bootstrap**, with 50,000 resamples and seed `20260925`. Sample ten recording indices with replacement, carry both systems’ components together, and recompute each corpus ratio and its difference. Report the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles, alongside the individual deltas and ten leave-one-recording-out influence calculations without retraining. If source grouping reveals dependence, resample source groups instead. Do not bootstrap clips, frames, or speaker turns as independent observations. With only ten recordings, overlapping CV training sets, and one training seed, this is a descriptive conditional interval—not a guaranteed 95% population-confidence statement or a measurement of training randomness. CV-error dependence makes naive uncertainty calculations unreliable. [Bengio and Grandvalet](https://jmlr.csail.mit.edu/papers/v5/grandvalet04a.html)

Register correctness gates before empirical scores: verified recording separation; valid reference intervals; preserved scoring boundaries; exact whole-file durations; full recording coverage; no reference activity or speaker counts supplied to inference; finite probabilities; nonoverlapping final hypotheses; correct window timestamp accounting; and successful synthetic global-identity tests involving more than eight speakers. Enforce the owner’s single-GPU, ≤20 GB profile on training and full-recording inference. Missing predictions must fail evaluation, not silently remove difficult recordings. Since every training recording exceeds eight speakers, none may be excluded as an exceptional capacity case. Predicted speaker count alone is not evidence of successful linking; inspect matched speech duration and confusion under the single recording-wide mapping.

My proposed **deployment adoption gate**, chosen prospectively rather than from data, requires all of the following: primary corpus improvement of at least 2.0 percentage points; negative macro difference; improvement on at least seven of ten recordings; no recording worse by more than 5.0 points; conditional bootstrap upper endpoint below zero; and no corpus regression at zero collar. These are conservative operational tolerances, not a formal significance test. If any fails, retain the calibrated frozen pipeline as the deployment default and report the fine-tuned result honestly. Still preserve the all-10 experimental model. Claiming a benefit specifically from weight adaptation additionally requires supporting results in the fixed-threshold comparison.

The decoder is a reasonable prediction-derived approximation, with an important defect and an unavoidable limitation. ## Data prep and fine-tuning recipe (training-recipe)

There are material blockers. The sample will reject most of the actual training annotations, its checkpoint selection does not measure the deployment task, and its smoke gate does not establish compliance with the 20 GB budget. Arrival-order labels and silence-only targets are supported by the inspected NeMo implementation; those are not inherently broken. I recommend annotation repair followed by conservative partial fine-tuning and nested recording/group-level validation. No GPU training was executed in this audit.

The dataset archive is now present locally, superseding the brief’s download blocker. Running the sample parser against its ten annotation files found **109 zero-length turns, five reversed intervals, and 27 pairs of positive-duration turns overlapping across different speakers**. The immediate blocker is the incompatibility between these annotations and the strict checks at [data.py:111](/tmp/claude-1004/-mnt-sdb-arafat-ehz-llm/3a52e9fe-0a44-4844-a7b8-28b1281aacff/scratchpad/src/src/data.py:111) and `data.py:127`. Review affected audio, retain immutable originals, and record each correction with its reason. Until repaired, uncertain regions should be excluded from training supervision rather than silently becoming negative speech targets. Any corrected evaluation references need a separate version and declared protocol. The paper’s single-label overlap policy does not explain away the actual contradictions. [Bengali-Loop annotation protocol](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.14291v1#S4.SS2)

Clip construction is mostly mechanically sound. `data.py:144–147` clips turns to the audio interval and rebases timestamps correctly. `data.py:172–192` recursively halves intervals while preserving sample-level coverage and enforcing at most eight distinct reference speakers. My targeted checks passed for coverage, relative timestamps, silence retention, and speaker limits. The function does allow a final clip almost one second longer than `max_seconds`, as documented at line 189, and does not guarantee a minimum duration for every recursive leaf. More importantly, the subdivision runs for **both train and dev**, at `data.py:320`, despite the module docstring saying otherwise. Reference-aware development clips can support a diagnostic loss, but they cannot establish performance on reference-free full recordings.

The actual local speaker distribution changes the design priorities. Counting positive-duration intervals, while retaining conflicting overlaps for this diagnostic, fixed 30-second windows contain at most **six** speakers: 365 windows contain one speaker, 539 contain two, and only seven contain five or six. No tested fixed 30-, 60-, or 105-second window exceeded eight speakers. Thus recursive halving is not currently the main training path, although more-than-eight-speaker **recording-level linking remains essential**. Recompute these counts after annotation repair and for randomly offset crops. The 30-second distribution also means channels seven and eight receive no positive supervision; repeated fine-tuning can suppress those channels. Recursive halving would further shorten and overweight crowded regions under uniform-per-clip sampling and mean-per-clip validation loss.
