Exploratory session-architecture measurements — council round 3

Material finding: H2 is partly supported, but its “cleaner tracks” premise is refuted on this diagnostic subset. Medium native sessions reduce cross-session fragmentation enough to improve real linking, while worsening within-session speaker tracking. H1 correctly identifies a large linking bottleneck, but neither the existing oracle nor these results justify saying fine-tuning failure was predetermined by architecture. Oracle distinction is material. The existing oracle assigns each track its majority-overlap reference identity independently, including core and context. It can merge multiple channels inside the same session, violating the practical linker’s cannot-link rule and repairing local fragmentation. To measure tracking without that repair, the primary diagnostic also uses a maximum-overlap one-to-one channel/reference assignment separately in each session; unmatched tracks keep unique identities. It preserves each local channel’s whole trajectory and only aligns identities across sessions. Assignment is based on pre-decoder overlap, so this is a constrained diagnostic, not a mathematically optimized post-gap-fill DER lower bound. Verified implementation: [bnd/link.py:810](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/bengali-diarization-nemotron/bnd/link.py:810), [bnd/link.py:447](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/bengali-diarization-nemotron/bnd/link.py:447). Our mapping is in score_sessions.py:strict_labels.

Interpretation of H1: at 20 seconds, replacing practical linking by the constraint-preserving oracle reduces exploratory DER by 10.85 percentage points, almost entirely confusion. That is a large architecture bottleneck, but the remaining 15.71% is not negligible: 12.18 points are speech errors and 3.53 are local confusion. The existing many-to-one oracle overstates the clean cross-session opportunity by 2.19 points here; on train_006 alone its extra local repair is 6.16 points. Gap fill is also identity-dependent, so real-minus-oracle differences are not an exact causal attribution to WeSpeaker alone. The within-session model affects fragmentation, purity, embeddings, and which merges cannot-link prevents; it does not merely supply a fixed speech mask. “Fine-tuning does not help” was not mathematically predetermined, and the prior no-demonstrated-gain finding applies to that recipe and architecture.

The no-gap-fill diagnostic also degrades: exploratory strict-oracle confusion is 3.24%, 3.93%, 4.32%, 4.60%, 5.78%, 11.87% for 20/60/120/180/300/full, respectively. Full component scores with and without gap fill are retained in results.json and scores.csv. The observed local degradation therefore is not solely an interaction with gap filling.

Interpretation of H2: the prespecified 120-second practical system improves exploratory pooled DER by 2.49 points despite worsening strict local DER by 1.67 points. Its remaining practical-to-strict gap is 6.68 points. The 180-second result is nearly the same overall, but 300 seconds loses the benefit. Improvement is heterogeneous: train_006 improves by 8.48 points at 120 seconds while train_001 slightly worsens; at 180 seconds train_001 improves but train_006 gives some benefit back. This is evidence for a tradeoff worth testing in inner folds, not an established universal optimum or a deployable performance claim.

Thus the medium tracks are fewer and longer, but not purer. At 120 seconds the exploratory number of linker tracks falls by 72.7%, mean owned speech per track grows by 3.27 times, and core purity falls by 2.02 percentage points. Speaker overcount shrinks, but count accuracy alone is insufficient: the 300-second system has still fewer predicted speakers while DER is worse. A mixed session track cannot be repaired by any linker that treats the track as indivisible. Conversely, median purity remains high because numerous small clean tracks can conceal a few long contaminated tracks; duration-weighted purity is the relevant countercheck.

Capacity-only explanation is insufficient. Exploratory native inputs above reference capacity / all sessions are 0/540, 0/182, 0/137, 0/92, 1/61, and 2/38 for 20/60/80/120/180/300 seconds. At 120 seconds, every core plus context fits within eight reference speakers, yet strict confusion rises from 3.53% to 5.02%. At 300 seconds, reference speech outside the per-core top eight is only 0.178% of reference speech, while strict confusion is 7.01%. These reference counts diagnose capacity only; they were not supplied to inference or real linking.

Exploratory capacity-trigger association: using at least one second of p>=0.40 activity on the seventh or eighth channel flags 2/92 sessions at 120 seconds, 4/61 at 180 seconds, and 7/38 at 300 seconds. At 120 seconds, 270.14 of 275.62 minority-overlap seconds occur in unflagged sessions; at 300 seconds, 253.07 of 353.27 do. These totals overlap when predicted tracks overlap and are impurity diagnostics, not DER seconds. This trigger misses most observed contamination. A trigger at mere channel activation would be noisier; its behavior was not measured. This analysis does not pretend that cutting cached probabilities simulates resetting AOSC.

Proposed, untested reset policy: keep cumulative confident occupancy in final output-channel order, debounce use of the last channels, close the current session at a chunk boundary when near capacity, and also impose a maximum session age to cover drift without saturation. Export the completed session’s probabilities/tracks, create a fresh state with init_streaming_state, clear cumulative predictions/occupancy, then resume feature chunks with correct context and offsets. Give each session its own local channel namespace and link completed tracks through the existing embedder. A small overlap/replay buffer and single ownership of frames can stabilize the boundary, but require validation. Do not merely zero spkcache: FIFO, silence profile, permutation and output accumulation must reset together. The state fields are visible in modules/sortformer_modules.py:31–60, and prediction permutation correction in that file:1000–1006. Occupancy is an imperfect proxy for remaining identity capacity because fragmentation can consume channels and confusion can merge speakers before the last channels appear.

The current offline configuration commits chunks about every 27.2 seconds with about 3.2 seconds of right context (340 and 40 encoder frames, subsampling 8, feature hop 10 ms; chunk arithmetic in sortformer_modules.py:294–319). A reset can therefore react only after an already processed chunk; it cannot undo that chunk’s mistakes without replay. A lower-latency streaming configuration would change this granularity and needs separate validation. No adaptive-reset inference run or performance gain is claimed here.

Fine-tuning inference, not a measured new result: medium sessions expose a real identity-learning target, especially return-to-speaker stability and fragmentation. At 120 seconds, train_006 has exploratory strict local confusion of 12.21% versus 9.26% at 20 seconds, with only a 3.09-point remaining practical-to-strict DER gap. Better within-session tracking could therefore matter materially in that recording and could also yield cleaner embedding averages. The existing training already draws sessions up to 105 seconds (bnd/train.py:55 and 249–261); merely making the inference window longer or repeating the same BCE training does not establish a new learning target.

Minimal objective change to test: replace the all-frame activity BCE for the tracking experiment with masked conditional speaker-identity cross-entropy on confident acoustically voiced, unambiguous reference interiors. Keep one arrival/permutation mapping for the entire session, not a fresh mapping per chunk. Exclude pause-only label regions and ambiguous coarse boundaries from the loss; the mask can come from a frozen base VAD intersected with single-speaker reference interiors, using training recordings only. Keep the speech gate and label-convention decoder fixed during the comparison, so a score change must come from identity assignment rather than filling pauses. Full-sequence logits are exposed by the streaming return_logits path; the current target alignment and all-frame BCE calls are at [third_party/nemo-speech/nemo/collections/asr/models/sortformer_diar_models.py:1651](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/bengali-diarization-nemotron/third_party/nemo-speech/nemo/collections/asr/models/sortformer_diar_models.py:1651), and [third_party/nemo-speech/nemo/collections/asr/losses/bce_loss.py:85](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/bengali-diarization-nemotron/third_party/nemo-speech/nemo/collections/asr/losses/bce_loss.py:85) currently masks only padding. This needs a loss/mask plumbing change, not a new model head. Preserve reference speaker identities throughout each contiguous session and favor samples where a speaker returns after intervening speakers. Extending contiguous draws toward the intended inference duration and upweighting return frames are sensible subsequent controlled changes, not prerequisites to the minimal objective ablation. The current loader sometimes splices two chunks (audio_to_diar_label.py:1189–1209), which should be controlled when testing long-delay natural tracking. No gain from this training change has been measured.
