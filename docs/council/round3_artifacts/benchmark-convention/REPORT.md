H4 is materially supported for pause inclusion and boundary decoding, but not for unrestricted turn expansion. A defensible benchmark mode is the frozen zero-shot system with same-speaker gap filling at 5 seconds and flooring both final boundaries to whole seconds. On the ten recordings this changes DER from **26.9699% to 24.9067%** (−2.0632 percentage points). These are **exploratory looks**, not a new independent performance claim. Extending the gap threshold has much smaller value: a follow-up integer sweep finds 7 seconds at 24.7236%, only 0.1831 points below 5 seconds; inner argmin selection gives 24.8133%, while conservative inner 1-SE selection gives 24.9713%. There is no robust reason here to replace 5 seconds with aggressive filling.

FT already learned much of the pause-inclusive and early-onset convention. Its frozen DER is 27.6875%; flooring both boundaries gives 27.2724%, whereas flooring only offsets gives 26.6845%. The main decoder comparison with proper inner-fit sources gives 24.9713% for ZS versus 26.7422% for FT under 1-SE selection. Thus convention decoding strengthens, rather than reverses, the absence of a demonstrated FT advantage in this architecture. The exact historical human timestamping operation cannot be recovered from these labels and detector traces: “annotators always floor both timestamps” is stronger than the evidence permits.

Everything in this directory was produced on CPU. No model was trained, no repository file was edited, no GPU or subagent was used, and no external predictions were submitted. The source repository is `/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/bengali-diarization-nemotron`. The compact overview is [convention_summary.png](convention_summary.png), with an exportable [PDF](convention_summary.pdf). Scripts, per-recording scores, selected RTTMs, six WAV excerpts, figures, and input provenance are retained here.

**Verified protocol versus empirical reconstruction.** The [Bengali-Loop paper, §4.2–4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.14291v1#S4.SS2) describes fully manual annotation, recording-local identities, new turns when a different speaker begins, first-speaker ownership of overlap, and HH:MM:SS CSV times. It does not specify floor versus nearest rounding, a minimum acoustic duration, or a numerical pause cutoff. These omissions matter: the following numerical characterization is inferred from the supplied training release and audio-derived measurements, not an undocumented procedure attributed to the annotators.

The code preserves valid CSV rows in file order for scoring, dropping impossible rows; training merges overlapping/touching same-speaker rows ([labels.py:7](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/bengali-diarization-nemotron/bnd/labels.py:7)). Scoring unions positive same-speaker overlaps, keeps collars at original row boundaries, uses whole-file UEM, and scores cross-speaker overlap ([score.py:221](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/bengali-diarization-nemotron/bnd/score.py:221)). These are verified implementation facts. They are not necessarily identical to the competition's unpublished exact scorer.

The adjacent-row gap statistics are explicitly row statistics, not a union-of-speech timeline. Nested rows explain why 15 negative adjacent gaps coexist with 27 intersecting cross-speaker pairs. The many genuine positive gaps rule out a general “speaker owns the entire timeline until the next speaker” convention. Among the 1,313 one-second gaps between different-speaker rows, both cached detectors mark 641.93 seconds as speech and 459.31 seconds as non-speech; the remainder is detector disagreement. This mix is consistent with coarse timestamps and small pauses, not universally silent or universally filled gaps. Conversely, the 42 same-speaker row gaps longer than five seconds contain 737 seconds, of which 672.25 seconds are detector-silent. Reuniting all appearances of a speaker would erase deliberately separated turns. These figures describe row-adjacent intervals and can include residual nested-reference defects; no acoustic truth is asserted.

**Flooring is a good output transform, not a proved universal annotation algorithm.** I reproduced the earlier nearest-transition diagnostic using frame-center resampling: pooled median detector minus annotation times are +0.23 seconds for onset and +0.16 seconds for offset when both detectors must be active. This matching method treats every row endpoint as a potential speech/silence edge, even at speaker changes; VAD cannot establish a speaker-change boundary. It also selects the nearest transition within ±1.5 seconds, potentially matching an intra-turn syllable or pause.

A stricter check uses speech-union boundaries separated from the adjacent union interval by ≥1 second, requires ≥160 ms of unchanged detector state on both sides, and still matches within ±1.5 seconds. **Decoder experiment design and checks.** The main experiment holds each fold's selected session, activity threshold, clusterer, and cluster threshold fixed. It uses 37 low-parameter output rules listed in [decoder_rules.json](decoder_rules.json) and specified in [DECODER_SPEC.md](DECODER_SPEC.md). Selection uses each outer fold's eight inner recordings. FT predictions come from fits excluding both the outer fold and the recording's inner fold, via the assertion-checked source function [p3b.py:366](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/bengali-diarization-nemotron/bnd/p3b.py:366). This is 40 recording–fit observations per system, not 40 independent recordings. We report argmin and a paired recording 1-SE rule, with a deterministic preference for fewer transformations and smaller thresholds. Upstream settings are not retuned jointly with the new decoder; those settings were selected under the old decoding convention.

The initial rules compare unchanged/whole-second floor/nearest rounding/ceil/−0.5-second shift; onset-only or offset-only floor; same-speaker silent-gap limits 0/2/5/10/20/60; final short-turn deletion at 0.25/0.5/1/2 seconds; A–B–A absorption for brief interruptions; and filling ≤1/2-second gaps across different speakers by extending the previous speaker. Extra duration rules are tested with and without floor. A separate cache experiment changes the winner in coactive frames from maximum probability to earliest predicted active onset. A disclosed follow-up checks integer gap limits 0–20, 30, and 60 because g5 and g10 were close. There is no per-recording oracle threshold in the reported inner-selected results.

Outer pre-gap outputs were reconstructed from cached tracks and fresh CPU execution of the unchanged linker. Reapplying the original gaps reproduces each frozen score within 1e−8 DER. The fast exact event-integration scorer supports multilabel references and exclusive hypotheses. It was checked against the repository's pyannote scorer on **180 system × recording × transform × collar cases**; maximum MISS/FA/CONF/reference-time difference was **9.1e−13 seconds** ([scorer_validation.json](scorer_validation.json)). A further 36 randomized synthetic overlap/collar comparisons also passed; the final artifact verification passed 272 assertions, including source hashes and paired-fit exclusions. Collars are total widths 0, 0.25, and 0.5 seconds. Hypothesis boundary shifts never move the reference or its collars.

Both definitions are explicit here. Flooring is not equivalent to a constant timing shift: it also collapses short turns and enforces the label grid. It drops 702 zero-bin-length ZS frozen turns, comprising 142.71 seconds of their original intervals, and 891 FT turns comprising 159.60 seconds. ZS output speaker-count sum falls from 357 to 326; FT from 323 to 299. This is partly deletion of brief labels, not improved speaker recognition. None of these numbers measures whether the deleted speaker activity was acoustically real.

For ZS, floor changes MISS/FA/CONF from **6.9005 / 6.8503 / 13.2191%** to **6.1495 / 6.0310 / 12.7262%**. The gap occupancy explains the plateau. **First-speaker decoding was tested directly on cached tracks.** The current decoder chooses highest probability and uses earliest onset only to break probability ties ([link.py:74](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/bengali-diarization-nemotron/bnd/link.py:74)). I reversed those priorities while preserving local activity, linking, owned cores, and each frozen gap. Earliest onset refers to the active predicted bout visible in the local session/context; it is an implementable proxy, not access to the true first speaker.

On all ten recordings, the winner changes for 122.11 seconds in ZS and 146.57 seconds in FT. With floor, ZS changes **24.9067 → 24.9821%**, FT **27.2724 → 27.2904%**. Every ZS inner fold selects maximum-probability + floor over earliest-onset alternatives. FT inner argmins sometimes select earliest onset, but no FT 1-SE choice does. The first-speaker convention is real in the documentation; this naive implementation does not improve the benchmark and should not replace the frozen owner rule. Both systems suppress secondary speakers by construction, so these references cannot establish acoustically accurate overlap handling.

There is also a representational reason to keep snapping outside the neural model. The loader samples arbitrary frame-offset chunks ([audio_to_diar_label.py:885](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/bengali-diarization-nemotron/third_party/nemo-speech/nemo/collections/asr/data/audio_to_diar_label.py:885)) and can concatenate two chunks ([audio_to_diar_label.py:1209](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/bengali-diarization-nemotron/third_party/nemo-speech/nemo/collections/asr/data/audio_to_diar_label.py:1209)). Without the original absolute-second phase, exact global floor snapping cannot be inferred deterministically from the local waveform. A model can learn an average onset bias and turn continuity; the exact output time grid is a decoder responsibility. This is inference from the verified loader behavior.

A fair future test would jointly reselect the target recipe and the final decoder inside the inner folds, comparing raw benchmark targets against the existing onset-shift recipe, and comparing both to frozen base + fixed gap5/floor. Retain reliable pause-inclusive targets, explicitly audit excluded long-pause rows before adding them, and keep corruption exclusions separate from desired turn continuity. Train any acoustic release target on independently corrected speech boundaries and overlap labels; blindly restoring the 52-second detector-silent row is not a principled target improvement. Use equal inference/linking settings, equal budgets, recording-level splits, and an untouched external labelled evaluation. No numerical prediction of the new fine-tune's DER is justified by the available cache.

**Practical release versus benchmark mode.** For a practical diarizer, the recommended default is the base system's full-resolution speech activity with no multi-second silent-gap filling and no whole-second snap. Preserve brief utterances. Merge truly touching segments of the same predicted identity, and expose optional conversational turn grouping separately from the underlying speech intervals. Keep overlap evidence available; if an ASR application needs a single speaker at a time, expose that as a derived exclusive view. Do not imply that first-speaker suppression estimates all acoustic speakers. The best acoustic threshold, overlap output, and short-pause smoothing still require independent fine-resolution validation; the present benchmark cannot establish them. “10 ms output” is temporal resolution, not a claim of 10 ms accuracy.

For `benchmark_mode=bengali_loop`, retain the frozen ZS upstream configuration **s20 / avg / θ_act 0.40 / θ_clu 0.60**, fill only silent gaps ≤5 seconds bounded by the same predicted speaker, floor both final endpoints, drop zero-length snapped turns, and merge touching identical labels. Keep the maximum-probability exclusive owner because the tested onset-priority substitute failed. Record this convention explicitly in output metadata. Do not extend across another speaker, impose a 1- or 2-second acoustic minimum, or merge speaker identities by turn duration. G7 is a reasonable exploratory candidate for future validation, not a reason to replace the simple g5 recommendation now. Applying the same benchmark mode to FT needs separate calibration; the data favor offset-only floor for FT, and no FT release advantage has been demonstrated.

The conflict is measurable without pretending to possess acoustic gold. Against independent Silero activity, ZS's all-ten speech-activity F1 changes from **90.92% without gap fill → 81.80% with the frozen g5 decoder → 78.70% with g5+floor**. FT is **79.86% without gap fill and 78.67% after flooring its frozen output**. These are detector-agreement proxies, not acoustic accuracy scores. Nevertheless, a large DER improvement accompanied by substantially worse independent VAD agreement makes it inappropriate to advertise the gain as improved physical speech timing. Fractional activity and benchmark turns serve different needs and should be separate outputs.

The deliverable depends on existing cached probabilities, tracks, embeddings, FT fits, and phase-3b upstream selections. It does not depend on a new result from another council role. Better linking may change the benefit of same-speaker gap filling, because a correct identity reunion makes more gaps eligible; all decoder results are conditional on the current linker. The audio-proxy audit does not determine true overlap owners, speaker aliases, or exact phonetic boundaries. Several earlier looks informed the experiment design; even its inner-selected outer results remain exploratory. Conditional bootstrap intervals do not account for that reuse. Whole-second snapping can delete real short speech and speaker identities, and long turn filling is unsuitable for default acoustic output.

Open questions needing the owner or data authors are the original timestamping tool and whether it displayed floor/nearest/inclusive seconds, the exact Kaggle scorer and overlap/collar policy, and whether independently annotated fine-resolution Bengali speech/overlap data can be obtained. A new convention-aligned FT comparison and an external evaluation remain prospective work, not completed evidence. Within this lens, the material missing baseline is **simple schema-aware decoding**, especially flooring; elaborate minimum-duration, gap, merge, and overlap-owner heuristics did not reveal another substantial gain.
